# Art direction: the pin-up wall

Status: shipped 2026-11-11. Critic scores by pass: 5, 6, 7.5, 7.75, then 8.0 with no high findings. Blurred guess: "a small indie product studio, likely news or civic apps."

## The world
A studio's wall of finished apps. The page is the wall. Every real screen is a paper sheet held up with banana tape. The headline and the studio note are pinned sheets too. On a desktop with a mouse, a work lamp follows the pointer, and each sheet's shadow swings away from it.

## Why this world (4-part concept test)
1. It is a real place: a workbench wall where a small studio pins up what it shipped.
2. It holds the real data: every sheet is a real screenshot, and the key below names each app, its proof and its status.
3. It blurs well: taped sheets on a warm wall read as "a studio's wall of things it made."
4. Only this brand fits it: banana tape and the banana lamp light.

## Directions tried
- A. Pin-up wall (chosen).
- B. One day, hour by hour, with a "now" line. Rejected: most app times would be invented, and it copies DayStart's morning world.
- C. Ship log ledger beside one screen. Rejected: it is a generic changelog template and fails the blur test.

## Rules
- Wall: #EFE8D9 light, #12100B dark. Sheets: paper #FBF8F0. Tape: banana #FFD23F with torn ends.
- One sans: Hanken Grotesk (Nate's choice, 2026-11-11).
- Real screens only. Never edit them or invent data. No user numbers.
- Pointer as lamp: sheet shadows swing away from it, no glow. Fine pointers only, off for touch and reduced motion.
- OG image: og-2026-11b.png, with the headline sheet pinned among real screens.

## What the critic still flags (mediums, next polish)

## The wall is touchable (2026-11-11)
Nate: "it's not funny ... it'd be really funny if somehow there's more tape ... you could move it around."
Three iterations, screenshots in `docs/tape-2026-11/`:
- A. Pick up any sheet and move it (shipped). Mouse grabs anywhere; touch only by the tape, so the page still scrolls. The sheet hangs from its tape, swings while carried and settles with a wobble. Every re-stick adds one more strip, corners first, up to six. The wall label stays where the sheet was hung. "Put everything back" appears after the first move. The first time DayStart scrolls into view it stirs once, like a draft.
- B. Over-taped wall with studio odds and ends (sticker, sticky note, release slip, coffee ring). Rejected: decoration to look at, and it crowds the work.
- C. Tape gun: click bare wall to slap strips, the roll runs thin, the roll tidies. Rejected: the joke is about the tape, not the work, and the roll reads as a control.
Why A: the joke comes from what you do to the real screens, and B's best idea (way too much tape) arrives as the consequence of playing.
Critic: 7 (fix), then 9 (ship), no high or medium open. Rules: no saved positions; Reduce Motion keeps it movable with no swing; dragging is a bonus, every sheet stays a focusable link.
Open lows: the reset link sits under the wall, a screen below the top sheets; dropped sheets can cover other labels until reset.
